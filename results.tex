\section*{Results}

\subsection*{4.1 Experimental Overview}

Eight model configurations were evaluated on a held-out test subject (case 000) representing a patient
with cerebrovascular pathology not seen during training.  The configurations arise from the full factorial
combination of two task modes --- \emph{domain translation} ($\times 1$, hereafter \textbf{Denoising})
and \emph{joint domain-translation and super-resolution} ($\times 2$, hereafter \textbf{Super-Resolution})
--- two network architectures --- \emph{Original} (SR4DFlowNet baseline) and \emph{CBAM-PreUpsampling}
(pre-upsampling attention extension) --- and two input conditioning strategies:
\emph{Masked} training, where ground-truth supervision is restricted to the Circle of Willis mask
$\mathcal{M}$, and \emph{Synthetic-noise} training, where the 7T target is predicted from an
unmasked 3T input augmented with simulated acquisition noise. Table~\ref{tab:experiment_map} summarises
the eight configurations together with their abbreviated identifiers used throughout the results.

\begin{table}[h!]
  \centering
  \caption{Summary of the eight evaluated configurations.  `Masked' indicates mask-guided training with
  the Circle of Willis target restricted to $\mathcal{M}$; `Synth.\ noise' indicates training from
  unmasked 3T input with synthetic noise augmentation.  Velocity and background metrics were not
  computed for the two CBAM-PreUpsampling / Synth.\ noise experiments (indicated by~---) because
  these runs were evaluated after the primary analysis pipeline had been completed.}
  \label{tab:experiment_map}
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{clll}
    \toprule
    ID & Task mode & Architecture & Input strategy \\
    \midrule
    A & Denoising ($\times 1$) & Original           & Masked \\
    B & Denoising ($\times 1$) & Original           & Synth.\ noise \\
    C & Denoising ($\times 1$) & CBAM-PreUpsampling & Masked \\
    D & Denoising ($\times 1$) & CBAM-PreUpsampling & Synth.\ noise \\
    \midrule
    E & Super-Resolution ($\times 2$) & Original           & Masked \\
    F & Super-Resolution ($\times 2$) & Original           & Synth.\ noise \\
    G & Super-Resolution ($\times 2$) & CBAM-PreUpsampling & Masked \\
    H & Super-Resolution ($\times 2$) & CBAM-PreUpsampling & Synth.\ noise \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Performance was assessed along three complementary axes: (i)~\textbf{velocity reconstruction fidelity},
quantified by the mean squared error of the predicted velocity field within the CoW mask
($\text{MSE}_{\text{vel}}$) and per-directional components ($u$, $v$, $w$); (ii)~\textbf{vascular
geometry fidelity}, assessed by re-segmenting each predicted output and comparing the resulting mask
against the ground-truth 7T mask using the Dice overlap coefficient, the symmetric mean surface
distance (sMSD), and the Hausdorff distance (HD$_{95}$); and (iii)~\textbf{background suppression},
measured by the MSE of predicted velocities outside the vascular mask
($\text{MSE}_{\text{bg}}$).  All metrics are reported averaged across nine cardiac frames.

Within the Denoising mode, masked training yields a clear advantage: configuration~A achieves the
lowest total velocity MSE (0.04281), outperforming the synthetic-noise counterpart~B (0.04721),
a relative improvement of 9.3\%.  CBAM-PreUpsampling with masked input (C) produces a comparable
result (0.04306), marginally worse than~A in total MSE but achieving lower component-wise error on
the $u$ direction (0.01327 vs.\ 0.01354).  The $v$-component is consistently the most challenging
across all configurations, a pattern that may reflect the left--right phase-contrast direction being
most susceptible to residual aliasing and partial-volume effects.

In the Super-Resolution mode, the absolute velocity MSE values are uniformly higher than their
Denoising counterparts, as expected given the additional upsampling task.  Configuration~G
(CBAM-PreUpsampling, masked) achieves the best total MSE (0.04844) among SR configurations,
outperforming both~E (0.04977) and~F (0.04983).  The gap between masked (E) and synthetic-noise (F)
configurations in SR is notably small (0.04977 vs.\ 0.04983), suggesting that for the harder $\times 2$
task, the architectural choice has a larger impact on velocity accuracy than the input conditioning
strategy.

\subsection*{4.3 Vascular Geometry Fidelity}
% -------------------------------------------------------

CoW segmentation pipeline applied to the original 7T data, and then comparing the resulting masks
against the ground-truth 7T segmentation.  Results for all eight configurations are reported in
Table~\ref{tab:geometry}.

The geometry results reveal the most pronounced performance gap between training strategies.  In the
Denoising mode, masked configurations (A and~C) substantially outperform their synthetic-noise
counterparts (B and~D) across all three geometry metrics.  Configuration~C achieves the highest
Dice score overall (0.855) and the lowest symmetric mean surface distance (0.183~mm), outperforming
the best original-model result (A, Dice = 0.843, sMSD = 0.202~mm).  Both masked models produce
predicted masks whose voxel count closely brackets the reference (17{,}544--18{,}027 vs.\ 15{,}577),
indicative of a slight but controlled over-segmentation.  By contrast, configuration~B (Original,
synth.\ noise) under-segments significantly (14{,}840 voxels), yielding a Dice of only 0.734 and a
Hausdorff distance of 15.09~mm --- almost double that of the corresponding masked model~A
(8.57~mm).

For the synthetic-noise Denoising case, the CBAM-PreUpsampling architecture (D) substantially
recovers geometric quality relative to the original architecture (B): Dice improves from 0.734 to
0.761 and HD drops from 15.09 to 9.86~mm, a 35\% reduction in worst-case boundary error.  This
suggests that the attention mechanism provides an architectural inductive bias that partially
compensates for the lack of mask-guided supervision.

In the Super-Resolution mode, the overall Dice scores are lower than for Denoising across all
configurations --- consistent with the harder task of recovering fine vascular structure from
$\times 0.5$-downsampled inputs --- but the relative ordering largely mirrors the Denoising findings.
Masked configurations (E and~G) achieve markedly lower HD values (9.01 and 9.50~mm) compared to
their synthetic-noise counterparts (F: 14.82~mm; H: 13.59~mm).  Notably, configuration~E (Original,
masked) achieves the highest Dice in the SR task (0.726), slightly surpassing~G (0.712); the
CBAM-PreUpsampling model predicts a larger mask volume (19{,}294 vs.\ 18{,}623 voxels), hinting at
increased over-segmentation of peripheral branches.

% -------------------------------------------------------
\subsection*{4.4 Background Suppression}
% -------------------------------------------------------

Accurate suppression of extravascular velocity predictions is clinically important both to prevent
spurious haemodynamic signals outside the vessel lumen and to ensure that downstream flow
quantification pipelines are not contaminated by background noise.  Table~\ref{tab:background}
reports the mean MSE of predicted velocity vectors in the background region (outside~$\mathcal{M}$)
for the six configurations for which background metrics were available.

Background suppression shows the starkest differentiation between training strategies.
In both task modes, masked training reduces the total background MSE by approximately 1.9--2.4$\times$
compared to synthetic-noise training (e.g., A: $6.44 \times 10^{-4}$ vs.\ B: $1.51 \times 10^{-3}$;
E: $7.83 \times 10^{-4}$ vs.\ F: $1.50 \times 10^{-3}$).  This directly reflects the mask-aware
loss formulation: by explicitly penalising background predictions with a total variation term
$\mathcal{L}_{\text{TV}}$, the masked training objective strongly encourages the network to suppress
extravascular signal, whereas the synthetic-noise strategy receives no equivalent regularisation
outside the vessel lumen.

Across all six measured configurations, the $v$-component (left--right direction) contributes the
largest share of background error, approximately two times higher than $u$ and $w$, consistent
with the velocity reconstruction findings and suggesting a direction-specific noise floor in the
3T acquisition.  Within masked configurations, CBAM-PreUpsampling consistently achieves lower
background MSE than the Original architecture in both modes (C vs.\ A; G vs.\ E), indicating
that the pre-upsampling attention mechanism improves spatial localisation of the predicted flow
signal.

Three principal conclusions emerge from the consolidated results.  \textbf{First, mask-guided
training is the dominant factor across all evaluation dimensions.}  Masked configurations
consistently outperform synthetic-noise configurations on geometry (Dice improvement of 9--15
percentage points in Denoising mode), background suppression ($\approx\!2.4\times$ lower
extravascular MSE), and --- in the Denoising task --- velocity reconstruction (up to 9\% MSE
reduction).  The effect is less pronounced for velocity MSE in Super-Resolution mode (0.04977
vs.\ 0.04983), where task difficulty may dominate over training strategy.

\textbf{Second, the CBAM-PreUpsampling architecture provides consistent but modest improvements
over the Original baseline under equivalent training conditions.}  In the Denoising masked
setting, CBAM-PreUpsampling improves Dice by 1.2 percentage points (0.843$\to$0.855), reduces
sMSD by 9\% (0.202$\to$0.183~mm), and lowers background MSE by 6\%
($6.44\to6.08 \times 10^{-4}$).  In the SR masked setting, the same architecture achieves the
lowest velocity MSE (0.04844) and background MSE ($6.71 \times 10^{-4}$), though at a small
cost in Dice relative to the Original model (0.712 vs.\ 0.726), attributable to marginal
over-segmentation of peripheral branches.  Notably, in the synthetic-noise Denoising setting
(D vs.\ B), the CBAM-PreUpsampling architecture substantially recovers geometric fidelity
(Dice: 0.761 vs.\ 0.734; HD: 9.86 vs.\ 15.09~mm) even without mask-guided supervision,
suggesting that the attention mechanism provides a beneficial spatial inductive bias.

\textbf{Third, the Super-Resolution task is uniformly harder than Denoising.}  Absolute Dice
scores drop by $\approx$13 percentage points and velocity MSE increases by 13--16\% across
corresponding configurations, consistent with the greater information loss involved in recovering
vascular structure from $\times 0.5$-downsampled inputs.  Despite this, all masked SR
configurations achieve sub-10~mm Hausdorff distances and velocity MSE values below 0.050,
demonstrating that meaningful haemodynamic reconstruction from clinically downsampled 3T
acquisitions is achievable within the NeuroFlow framework.
